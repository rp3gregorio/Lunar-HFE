# Audit report — 2026-07-03

*Full-repo audit: code critique, input inventory with bug-risk ranking, fixes
applied, and improvement recommendations for the guidebook and letter.
Requested focus: "where could the next Q_b-style or convergence-style bug
hide?" Every claim below is grounded in a file/line or an archived artifact.*

**Verdict up front:** the physics engine is sound (all invariants re-verified,
45/45 tests green), the published numbers are safe, and the audit found **one
real bug family** (solar-forcing construction, 10 callers), **one measurement
robustness defect** (single-shot brute timing), and **three latent traps** now
converted into loud guards or archived checks. All fixed this audit.

## 1. Findings

### F-1 (BUG, fixed): duplicated solar-forcing construction — 10 callers wrong

The exact disease that caused the Q_b bug, in a new organ. The solver applies
`(1 − albedo)` **internally** (`solver._surface_residual`: `radiative_in =
(1-albedo)*insolation`), so callers must pass the *raw* flux
`S0·cos(lat)·max(0, cos φ)`. Instead of one shared constructor, ~20 call sites
each built the forcing by hand — and they had drifted into two families:

| Family | Construction | Callers | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| correct | `S0·cos(lat)·max(0,cos φ)`, albedo passed separately | `retrieve_kd` (production), all `compute_*` sensitivity scripts, `compute_diviner_closure`, letter/results figures, tests | ✓ published numbers **unaffected** |
| wrong | `S0·(1−albedo)·clip(...)` — albedo **double-counted**, `cos(lat)` **omitted** (fictitious equatorial site) | `make_equilibrium_demo`, `make_{reconstruct,old_vs_new,newmethod,spinup}_animation`, `benchmark_speedup`, `equilibrium_demo.ipynb`, `05_animations.ipynb` | **fixed** |

Impact of the wrong family: net forcing off by ≈ −3 % (A15) / −8 % (A17) →
demo/animation temperatures ≈ 2–5 K below the true site values while labeled
"Apollo 15/17". The *stories* those artifacts tell (brute force converges onto
the shortcut; Step A/B mechanics) were internally self-consistent and remain
valid; the absolute temperatures were not site-accurate. The timing archive
was also measured under the wrong forcing.

**Fix (root cause, not symptoms):** new single constructor
`lunar.solver.standard_insolation(lat_deg, t)` with a docstring stating the
(1−A) convention; all 8 scripts + 2 notebooks now use it; every affected
artifact re-generated (animations, filmstrips, benchmark, demo overlay,
notebook). `05_animations.ipynb` was additionally carrying a stale
`KD = 4.58e-3` (pre-convergence-fix) → now 4.603.

### F-2 (robustness, fixed): the speed-up benchmark was not reproducible

Re-measuring the archive after the forcing fix exposed that the *brute* side
was a single-shot timing: 86 → 114 ms/lunation across two quiet-machine runs,
which alone moved the headline ratio 20× → 27×. `benchmark_speedup.py` now
takes the **median of 3** brute timings (matching the anchored side) and
archives the raw runs. Recommendation adopted throughout: quote the speed-up
as an order ("~20×"), never a fresh single-run decimal.
Final re-measured archive (quiet machine, corrected forcing, median-of-3):
**anchored 13.8 s; brute 279 s (median 93 ms/lun × 3000); 20× per solve;
61-solve sweep 14.0 min vs 4.7 h** — the 114 ms/lun run proved to be the
outlier and the ratio settles back at ~20×. Guidebook §speed-up updated to
match the archive.

### F-3 (latent trap → loud guard): the dense K_d grid can go stale silently

`config.KD_GRIDS` packs a dense ~0.2 mW band bracketing *today's* minima
(4.60 / 7.16). Any future input change (Q_b, χ, albedo…) that moves K_d\*
outside the band would silently degrade the vertex to coarse ~0.76 mW spacing
(±0.02 mW vertex noise — the exact bug fixed on 2026-07-01), with no warning.
**Fix:** `retrieve_kd.kd_star_from_residuals` now warns loudly when the
bracket spacing exceeds `KD_VERTEX_MAX_SPACING = 0.30 mW` or the minimum sits
at a grid edge (unit-tested on all three paths; bootstrap draws are exempted
so 1500 draws don't spam).

### F-4 (statistical hygiene, fixed + flagged): MCMC likelihood support

Two issues in `bayesian_crosscheck.py`:
1. The prior allowed K_d ∈ [1, 30] mW but the RMSE spline only has computed
   support on the sweep range — outside it (including when a low-Q_b draw
   inflates `kd_eff = kd·Q_b_pub/Q_b`) the Pchip was **extrapolating** an RMSE
   that was never computed. **Fixed:** the likelihood now returns −inf outside
   the sweep-grid support (`kd_eff_support`); cross-check re-run
   (P(A17>A15) = 91.4 %, contrast median +2.22, 95 % [−0.98, +5.50] —
   consistent with, and broader than, the bootstrap, as a cross-check
   should be).
2. `sigma_data = 0.5 K` is an **assumed** likelihood scale with no independent
   calibration — it sets the posterior width. Now documented in the docstring;
   the bootstrap CI remains the headline uncertainty. (Also fixed en route:
   the missing `corner` package that had been silently breaking `make aux`.)

### F-5 (coverage gap → archived check): dt and the spatial grid

`dt = 3600 s` and the grid (`dz0 = 2 mm`, growth 8 %) were the last two
production inputs with **no convergence artifact** — the same posture that hid
the n_inner bug. New `pipeline/compute/check_discretization.py` runs the
production solve against dt/2 and a refined grid (1 mm, 6 %) and archives the
sensor-band ⟨T⟩ shifts. Result (`results/discretization_check.json`):

* **refined grid: ≤ 23 mK → PASSED** (spatial discretization converged);
* **dt/2: 136 mK → FAILED the 50 mK criterion.** Halving the time step warms
  the sensor-band cycle mean by a nearly uniform +0.135 K — the third
  convergence-family finding in this project's history (outer cycles → F1;
  inner lunations → n_inner; now temporal resolution; most plausibly the
  dawn/dusk terminator under-resolved at 1-hour steps).

**Quantified impact (first-order refit against measured d⟨T⟩/dK_d):**
K_d\*(A15) would shift by **≈ −0.20 mW m⁻¹ K⁻¹** at dt = 1800 s. Context:
half the n_inner bias (0.4), 40× the plateau criterion (0.005), well inside
the bootstrap statistical width (σ_stat ≈ 0.56). Two structural mitigations:
the shift is nearly uniform in depth while K_d *tilts* the profile
(sign-changing sensitivity), so much of the dt effect lands in RMSE rather
than K_d bias; and it is common-mode across sites, partially protecting the
headline **contrast**.

**Decision required (published-number change — not made unilaterally):**
either re-baseline production at dt = 1800 s (doubles solve cost, ~14 → ~28 s;
sweep ~14 → ~28 min) with a dt/4 spot-check, then re-run the pipeline and
update both manuscripts — or keep dt = 3600 s and add an explicit
σ_dt ≈ 0.2 mW row to the error budget. **Recommendation: re-baseline** —
"every input convergence-tested" is this project's core credibility claim,
and the letter is still in preparation, so moving now is cheap.

### F-6 (minor, documented — not churned): time-grid endpoint duplication

Production builds `t = linspace(0, T_LUNAR, N+1)` — *inclusive* of both
endpoints, so the noon phase is double-weighted (1 part in 709) in cycle
means; some figure callers use `arange` (correct periodic sampling). At
sensor depths the bias is < 1 mK — harmless — but the split is untidy.
Recommendation: standardize on `arange(0, T_LUNAR, dt)` at the next
full re-run; **not** worth perturbing published numbers on its own.

### F-5 addendum II (the full dt story — three separable errors)

Deeper investigation while porting the solver revealed the "dt effect" was
THREE stacked issues, now separated experimentally
(`results/dt_kdstar_certification.json`, commensurate-grid ladders):

1. **Wrap sliver (~70 mK, fixed).** A plain `arange(0, T_LUNAR, dt)` grid does
   not tile the lunation exactly; the cycle chaining wraps with a phase sliver
   that distorts the effective forcing period. Fixed structurally:
   `lunar.solver.periodic_time_grid(dt_target)` builds a COMMENSURATE grid
   (`n·dt = T_LUNAR` exactly) — now the only allowed lunation grid; all 15
   pipeline callers + notebooks migrated. (This also retires the old
   inclusive-`linspace` double-counted-noon defect, F-6.)
2. **First-order surface ghost (~35 mK/halving, fixed).** The radiative march
   used the SAME endpoint Newton solution on both sides of the CN average
   (`T_s_prev = T_s_new`) — first-order by construction. Fixed: the explicit
   ghost now uses the previous step's stored surface temperature
   (trapezoidal, zero extra cost). Ladder improvement: 136 → 100 mK per
   halving. Tests green; brute/anchor share the fix.
3. **Residual O(dt) from frozen properties (~100 mK/halving at 3600,
   certified in K_d\* space).** K, ρ, c_p are evaluated at the explicit level
   (the standard linearisation) — removing this needs a per-step Picard
   iteration, a solver renovation deliberately NOT undertaken pre-publication.
   Instead, per this project's own doctrine (certify the quantity you
   report), the certification moved from ⟨T⟩ to **K_d\* directly**: dense-band
   mini-sweeps against the real Apollo sensors at commensurate dt=1800 vs
   900 measure |ΔK_d\*| — the number that sets production dt and an honest
   σ_dt error-budget row. The ⟨T⟩ shift is nearly uniform in depth and
   common-mode across sites, so K_d\* and especially the contrast converge
   far faster in dt than raw temperatures do.

### F-5 addendum III (the K_d\*-space certification FAILED at A17 → the MISSING WRAP STEP)

The "certify around it" strategy of item 3 was tested and **did not survive
contact with A17**. The wide-bracket commensurate ladder
(`results/dt_kdstar_certification_A17wide.json`) gives, at the Q_b=16
baseline:

| dt [s] | K_d\*(A17) [mW m⁻¹ K⁻¹] |
|---|---|
| 1800 | 7.699 |
| 900  | 7.397 |
| 450  | 7.251 |

Successive differences −0.302 / −0.146 mW (ratio 2.07): clean FIRST-order
convergence with Richardson limit **7.104 mW** — a +0.6 mW bias at dt=1800
(A15 is nearly immune, +0.008 mW per halving: its steeper deep gradient
makes K_d\* far less sensitive to the common-mode ⟨T⟩ offset). No affordable
dt reaches the 0.05 mW criterion, so the source had to be found and fixed.

**The forensic chain (all experiments preserved in the session log).** The
initial suspect — the frozen-property linearisation — was implemented as a
midpoint-Picard corrector in `_step` (`config.CN_PICARD_SWEEPS`) and
promptly EXONERATED as the driver: it fixed the surface cell's dt drift
(−44 → −8 mK/halving) but left the deep drift untouched (+48 → +55 mK). A
bisection sequence then eliminated, in order: the terminator kink (smooth
forcing: drift halves but stays first-order), property nonlinearity
entirely (constant-K/ρ/c_p march: still first-order), Picard depth (n=3 ≈
n=1), Newton tolerance (1e-4 vs 1e-11: identical), and the cold-start
transient (per-dt attractors from 6000-lunation spins: +61.3/+31.4 mK per
halving, ratio 1.95, depth-uniform — the PERIODIC ATTRACTOR itself was
first-order). A 25-cell toy replica of the scheme was second-order (ratio
4.01) while a single `_step` matched the toy bit-for-bit (0.0e0
difference) — pinning the defect in the MARCH, not the step.

**The bug (`solver.solve_pixel`, the wrap-sliver family's final form).**
The periodic time grid spans `[0, P−dt]` (no duplicated endpoint), so the
march's `k = 1..n_t−1` loop advances only `P−dt` seconds per cycle; chaining
cycles relabels the `t = P−dt` state as `t = 0` — a phase slip of exactly dt
per lunation. The earlier "wrap sliver" fix (commensurate grid, item 1) had
only shrunk this slip from ~6243 s (arange remainder) to dt; the missing
final step `t[-1] → P` (forcing wraps to `insolation[0]`) was the actual
disease all along. **Fix:** the march now takes the wrap step at the end of
every cycle (its result is the next cycle's `t=0` state and surface
temperature); C++ port mirrored (`cpp/solver.cpp`), verification
max |T_py − T_cpp| = 1.4e−12 K; all 48 tests green (3 new Picard regression
tests included).

**Result:** the production attractor drift collapsed from +61.3/+31.4 mK per
halving to −8.6/−3.5 mK at `CN_PICARD_SWEEPS=1`, and the residual is the
one-iterate Picard lag: sweeps 1/2/4/8 give −8.6/−4.2/−2.2/−2.0 mK.
Production setting `CN_PICARD_SWEEPS = 2` (~2 mK from the fixed point,
~0.02 mW on K_d\*(A17), 3 tridiagonal solves per step). The midpoint-Picard
corrector stays: it is the correct treatment of the T-dependent properties
and the surface coupling, and it is what makes the post-wrap-fix scheme
second-order rather than merely less-wrong.

**Acceptance — PASSED** (`pipeline/compute/dt_ladder.py`,
`results/dt_kdstar_certification_{A15,A17}_wrapfix.json`):

| dt [s] | K_d\*(A17) | K_d\*(A15) |
|---|---|---|
| 1800 | 7.0795 | 4.5961 |
| 900  | 7.0975 | 4.5948 |
| 450  | 7.1045 | 4.5944 |

ΔK_d\*(A17) per halving: +0.018 / +0.007 mW; A15: −0.0013 / −0.0004 —
all far inside the 0.05 mW criterion. The wrap-fixed dt=450 value equals the
buggy scheme's Richardson limit (7.104) to FOUR decimals: two independent
discretizations agreeing on the continuum limit. **Certified production
settings: DT_STEP=1800, CN_PICARD_SWEEPS=2, wrap-fixed march.**

**Fast path (same date).** The tripled per-step cost made the generic
Python march the pipeline bottleneck (~4.5 min/solve). `solve_pixel` now
dispatches the standard-Hayne case to a compiled kernel
(`solver._march_radiative_hayne`, a transcription of the verified C++ port;
`PixelInputs.hayne_params`), asserted equal to the generic path to <1e−9 K
by `test_fast_march_matches_generic`. One equilibrium solve: **1.0 s**
(~270×). Non-standard property models (3layer, martinez) keep the generic
path. The headline set comes from the full pipeline re-run at the certified
settings.

### F-9 (WRONG MODEL, found 2026-07-03 evening): the K_d–Q_b degeneracy is not proportional

While propagating the corrected numbers, the letter's degeneracy claims were
re-derived instead of trusted — and failed. Three artifacts encoded the
analytic assumption that the deep profile is invariant under
`(K_d, Q_b) → (αK_d, αQ_b)`:

1. `retrieve_kd.py`'s `qb_sensitivity` block (builds `K_d(αQ_b) = α·K_d`
   grids analytically — no solves);
2. `bayesian_crosscheck.py`'s likelihood (evaluates the RMSE spline at
   `K_eff = K_d·Q_b_pub/Q_b` — the same proportional surrogate);
3. the letter's §degeneracy sentences ("substituting the lower ~10 value
   rescales K_d downward to ~5.4 … the quantity constrained tightly is the
   product Q_b/K_d").

**Direct test (fast-path re-retrievals, `results/qb_degeneracy.json`):** the
invariance is false. Scaling (Q_b, K_d) by α = 10/16 at A17 shifts the whole
sensor band by a depth-uniform **−1.26 K** — the anchor's cycle-mean carries
a Q_b/K-dependent offset through the skin column, and the sensors constrain
absolute temperature, not just the gradient. The measured K_d*(Q_b):

| Q_b [mW m⁻²] | K_d\*(A15) | K_d\*(A17) | min RMSE (A17) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | — | 10.13 | 0.29 |
| 12 | — | 9.24 | 0.30 |
| 14 | — | 8.20 | 0.33 |
| 16 | 2.72 | **7.08** | 0.40 |
| 18/18.5 | 4.02 | 6.80 | 0.56 |
| 21 | **4.60** | — | — |
| 23.5 | 5.04 | — | — |
| 26 | 5.44 | — | — |

The two sites respond in OPPOSITE directions: A15 is gradient-dominated
(K_d\* rises with Q_b, roughly proportional in direction though not in
magnitude), A17 is absolute-T dominated (K_d\* falls as Q_b rises, turning
around near 20). This is the same mechanism that made A17 alone
hyper-sensitive to the wrap-step dt offset. Notably the A17 fit at Saito's
low flux is BETTER than at the published value (RMSE 0.29 vs 0.40): RMSE
alone cannot arbitrate the flux debate, which is the honest core of the
degeneracy story.

Consequences: (a) the letter's §degeneracy rescale numbers and the
"product Q_b/K_d" invariance sentence are replaced with the measured map;
(b) a corrected robustness statement becomes available — the contrast
K_d(A17)−K_d(A15) stays positive across the whole debated envelope even
with the two fluxes varied independently (worst pairing 6.80 − 5.44 =
+1.36 mW); (c) `bayesian_crosscheck.py` must be rewritten on a direct 2-D
(K_d, Q_b) RMSE surface (now affordable at ~1 s/solve) — until then its
marginal medians and the prior-width ordering percentages are wrong-model;
(d) `retrieve_kd`'s analytic `qb_sensitivity` block is superseded by
`pipeline/compute/compute_qb_degeneracy.py` (a permanent writer-script) and
should be removed or repointed at the direct map.

### Validity certificate at the new baseline (brute-vs-anchor control)

Question raised during the re-baseline: is the dt error an anchor-method
artifact, and does the method remain valid against brute force at dt = 1800?
Control experiment (`results/method_equivalence_dt.json`): brute force
(3000 lunations) and the anchor method, each at dt = 3600 and 1800 s, A15 at
the retrieved K_d\*.

* The dt shift is **identical** in both methods at every sensor depth
  (135.7 vs 135.8 mK at 0.8 m; agreement ≤ 0.3 mK) → the dt=3600 error lived
  in the **shared Crank–Nicolson stepper**, not in the anchor machinery;
  fixing dt fixes both methods equally.
* brute − anchor = −11 to −28 mK at both dts — within brute force's own
  3000-lunation approach floor, growing with depth exactly as the diffusive
  tail predicts, and **unchanged between time steps**: method equivalence is
  dt-independent.
* Wall-clock at dt = 1800: brute 754 s vs anchor 36 s (~21×) — the method's
  value doubles with the finer step.

pointing one way; single-source config with cited constants; writer scripts
for archives; loud certification instead of fixed spin-ups; tests that pin
