# Audit of `letter.tex` / `letter.pdf`

Date: 2026-06-22 UTC  
Scope: audit only. No edits made to `docs/letter/letter.tex` or `docs/letter/letter.pdf`.

## Headline

The contribution is clear in the first 200 words of the compiled PDF: the abstract states that the work retrieves meter-scale `K_d` separately at Apollo 15 and 17, gives the headline values, names the data source, and flags the `Q_b` conditionality. A reviewer reading only page 1 would understand both the novelty and the main result.

One presentation issue remains: the compiled `letter.pdf` is in `newonly` mode (`letter.tex:113`) with green highlighted insertions and line numbers (`letter.tex:173`). That is helpful for internal review but not submission-clean.

Recommendation: keep the abstract structure, but compile the submission from `letter_clean.tex` / `build_clean.sh` or switch to `clean` mode before sending to JGR.

## Methodological Completeness

Most Results claims are backed by figures, tables, or a direct pointer forward: `K_d` sweeps and bootstrap are tied to Figs. 6-7; solver certification is summarized in Section 2.3; `Q_b`/`H` degeneracy and the error budget are tied to Fig. 9 and Table 4; Diviner closure and Martinez diagnostics are tied to Figs. 10-11.

Two small inconsistencies should be fixed before submission. First, Table 2 reports Apollo 15 `K_d^*=4.57 [4.12,7.45]` (`letter.tex:826`) while the canonical retrieval JSON gives 4.58 and the surrounding Results text uses 4.58. Second, Section 2.5 still says each `K_d` uses “a full Crank--Nicolson spin-up” (`letter.tex:722`), while Section 2.3 correctly describes the flux-anchored equilibrium construction.

Recommendation: change Table 2 to 4.58, or relabel 4.57 as the RMSE-table site-fit value, and replace the Section 2.5 “full spin-up” sentence with “certified periodic-equilibrium solve.”

## Figure Parsimony

The figures are mostly load-bearing. Fig. 1 defines the measurement geometry and model contrast; Figs. 5-8 carry the core retrieval; Fig. 9 carries the degeneracy limitation; Fig. 10 carries the Martinez density diagnostic; Fig. 11 supports the Diviner closure. Captions are mostly self-contained and include enough context to be read independently.

The letter is dense for a short-format target: 11 figures, 4 tables, a long nomenclature table, and line-numbered review layout push the PDF to 24 pages. If JGR length pressure appears, the context map (Fig. 2) and possibly the Diviner closure (Fig. 11) are the least essential to the central proof, while Figs. 6-9 should not be cut.

Recommendation: keep Figs. 1, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 in the letter; move Fig. 2 and/or Fig. 11 to supplement only if page limits force a reduction.

## Reproducibility Statement

The Open Research section points to the Apollo HFE data DOI, Diviner/PDS source, and the public GitHub repository (`letter.tex:1761-1779`). The local git remote matches the cited repository URL. The statement also says a Zenodo DOI will be issued at acceptance.

The brief asks for a public repo and canonical results commit. The letter does not name a commit hash, tag, release archive, or Zenodo DOI yet. Without that, a reviewer can find the repository but cannot identify the exact results state.

Recommendation: add a frozen tag/commit hash for the canonical results, and replace the future-tense Zenodo sentence with the DOI once the release exists.

## Honest Limitations

The limitations are explicit and not buried. The abstract mentions the `Q_b` envelope reducing ordering probability to about 83%; Section 4.2 explains the `K_d/Q_b` degeneracy; Section 4.5 states that the 95% contrast is conditional on published basal fluxes and can be erased by the most adverse independent `Q_b` assignment. The `chi`/`H` conditionality is also separated from the quadrature budget in Table 4.

The only risk is interpretive phrasing: the abstract says the contrast “excludes zero” before qualifying with `Q_b`; the qualification follows immediately, but hurried readers may remember the stronger clause.

Recommendation: preserve the current limitation section, and consider adding “conditional” directly to the abstract contrast sentence.

## Citation Completeness

Most non-original claims are cited: HFE data and disturbances, Hayne and Martinez models, Diviner GCP, albedo/emissivity, basal fluxes, lunar site geology, porosity, lab conductivity, and model-selection practice all have references. The TSUKIMI mention is cited and the Open Research data sources have URLs/DOIs.

Potentially unsupported or thinly supported claims are small: “future microwave and terahertz sub-surface sounders all need” meter-scale `T(z)` is plausible but broad; “Chang'E and ChaSTE-class experiments” in the conclusion has no direct citation; “ALSEP gazetteer” appears as a table source without a bibliography entry.

Recommendation: add one citation or footnote for ALSEP coordinates and one for the Chang'E/ChaSTE landed-thermometry examples, or remove those examples from the conclusion.

## Voice

The scientific voice is mostly JGR-appropriate: precise, declarative, and cautious around identifiability. The strongest phrases are defensible because the text later narrows them, and the paper avoids marketing language.

Submission polish remains: comments/TODOs in the source (`letter.tex:138`, `letter.tex:177`) are harmless in the PDF but should not remain in a final archive source; `\date{\today}` (`letter.tex:170`) makes the PDF date non-reproducible; the current rendered PDF visibly shows green revision text and line numbers.

Recommendation: archive a clean, deterministic submission source/PDF with no revision color, no TODO comments, no `\today`, and AGU/JGR-required contribution/funding statements filled in.
